## 4. Flags and Recommendations

### F1 — [2] Brynjolfsson et al.: your local PDF is not the version of record ⚠ **action needed**

Your folder holds `w31161.pdf`, the **NBER working paper** (April 2023, revised November 2023). The citation in the thesis correctly points to the **QJE 2025 version of record**. These are not identical documents — the QJE version went through peer review and revision.

**Why this matters:** §1.2.1 and §1.5 quote "approximately 14%" and "more than five thousand agents" from the working paper. Before final submission, verify both figures against the QJE text, since the headline effect is reported slightly differently across versions.

**Recommendation:** keep the QJE citation (it is the correct version of record), obtain the QJE PDF through the NSBM library, and check the two figures. Do not change the reference — change your confidence in the quoted numbers until checked.

### F2 — [4] Mulyati et al.: page range missing

DOI, volume, issue and all author names are verified from the PDF. The article's **page range is not stated** in the citation. IIETA articles carry page numbers.

**Recommendation:** retrieve the page range from the IIETA article page and complete the entry. Low priority, but IEEE style expects it for a journal article.

### F3 — [18] Zhang et al.: preprint where a peer-reviewed version exists ⚠ **replacement recommended**

The cited arXiv:2406.11213 is a preprint. The same Peking University group has a **peer-reviewed successor in ACM Computing Surveys**, covering the same territory (183 articles, Jan 2020 – Dec 2024), which supports every claim you cite [18] for — field fragmentation and inconsistent evaluation practice.

**Recommended replacement:**
> L. Zhang, T. Jia, M. Jia, Y. Wu, A. Liu, Y. Yang, Z. Wu, X. Hu, P. S. Yu, and Y. Li, "A survey of AIOps in the era of large language models," *ACM Computing Surveys*, 2025, doi: 10.1145/3746635.

- Official page: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3746635 — *PDF access may require institutional access*
- Free PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.12472 (already downloaded as `arXiv_2507.12472.pdf`)

This is a strict upgrade: peer-reviewed venue, more recent, same authors, same argument. **Say the word and I'll apply the swap across the thesis.**

### F4 — [20] Liu et al.: weakest reference in the set ⚠ **decision needed**

arXiv preprint only — dblp indexes it as CoRR with no conference version. It sits beside three peer-reviewed prompt-injection references ([19] AISec '23, [21] NeurIPS 2024, [22] ICLR 2024).

It is **not strictly redundant** — the specific claim you draw from it (injection vulnerabilities found across a large proportion of *deployed, real-world* applications, via the HouYi black-box technique) is unique to this paper; [19] establishes the indirect vector and [21] evaluates in a synthetic environment.

**Two defensible options:**
1. **Keep it**, clearly marked as a preprint — justified because the empirical breadth claim has no peer-reviewed equivalent. *(My recommendation.)*
2. **Drop it** and let [19] and [21] carry §1.2.5 and §2.6.2. You lose the "large proportion of deployed applications" claim and would need to soften that sentence.

### F5 — [24] Hevner et al.: page-range discrepancy

Your citation gives **pp. 75–105**. The MIS Quarterly publisher page states **75–106**. Both circulate widely in the literature.

**Recommendation:** change to **pp. 75–106** to match the publisher of record.

### F6 — [1] VIGIL: a 2026 preprint carrying significant argumentative weight

[1] is not peer-reviewed, yet it is the basis on which you withdraw your categorical-novelty claim (§1.1) and position your contribution as complementary (§2.4.4).

**This is defensible and I recommend keeping it** — withdrawing an over-claim on the strength of a credible industrial preprint is more honest than retaining the over-claim. But state its preprint status where it carries the most weight, and check before final submission whether a peer-reviewed version has appeared.
