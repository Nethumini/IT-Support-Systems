# Chapter 6 - Discussion and Conclusions

## 6.1 Discussion of Key Findings

The results answer RQ1 by showing how evidence-grounded recommendations can be connected to deterministic control without allowing model text to become execution authority. In the implemented workflow, retrieved evidence supports a proposal, but a separate risk service determines the route; approval is bound to the action, parameters, and target; a registered contract supplies preconditions and postconditions; and the driver executes only a catalogue identifier. The 518 passing automated tests and the completed Windows trace show that these boundaries existed in the frozen artefact rather than only in its design. The contribution is therefore the coordinated workflow, not any individual retrieval, agent, risk, approval, or rollback mechanism.

For RQ2, the risk-adaptive condition prevented 36/36 repeated cases labelled unsafe for autonomous execution, while the approval-all condition executed 36/36. It also required 72 rather than 96 approval events, avoiding 24 approvals by allowing bounded low-risk candidates to proceed without a human token. This supports the intended middle position between no execution and uniform approval. However, the 100% risk and route agreement is specification conformance against researcher-assigned labels. It does not demonstrate that the weights, thresholds, or labels are universally correct. The paired comparison is more informative than the aggregate resolution percentages: the 19 unique scenarios executed under both B and C had identical verification verdicts and final states, while C blocked the 12 unsafe high-risk scenarios executed only by B.

RQ3 concerned outcome verification and recovery. Every injected fault that reached execution was detected: 18/18 under B and 15/15 under C, with none reported as success. Only three of six repeated rollback attempts were verified because one of the two unique rollback cases deliberately provided a recoverable prior state and the other did not. The latter escalated instead of claiming restoration. The real-Windows case strengthened this result: the disable command returned success, but the observed item remained enabled, producing `verified_failure`; recovery restored the saved fingerprint, consumed the backup, and produced `verified_success`. These observations show why command completion and issued rollback are insufficient outcome measures.

For RQ4, retrieval returned the labelled article at rank one for 30/30 queries, giving Hit@1 and Hit@3 of 1.000 and mean reciprocal rank of 1.000. Within this fixed knowledge base and query set, no retrieval error was observed. This result is deliberately narrow. Each query had one author-labelled relevant article and was supplied with its expected category, enabling the 0.05 category bonus. The outcome therefore demonstrates correct operation on the evaluated closed set, not general retrieval accuracy for unseen organisational knowledge or incorrectly classified requests.

## 6.2 Comparison with Literature

The findings support the literature synthesis that grounding, tool use, and safety enforcement solve different parts of the problem. RAG provides external evidence [15], and RAGAS emphasizes separate retrieval evaluation [6], but retrieval relevance does not authorize a system change. ReAct and AutoGen demonstrate reasoning, tools, and specialised agents [2], [3]; the present results support treating their outputs as proposals while retaining authority in independently testable services.

The adversarial cases are consistent with AgentDojo's warning that untrusted content can redirect tool-using agents [8]. AutoOps AI did not establish immunity from prompt injection. Instead, its deterministic catalogue and contracts contained the tested effects: text could not create approval or an unknown executable action. This is closer to runtime-enforcement work such as AgentSpec, GuardAgent, ShieldAgent, and ToolSafe [9], [16], [17], [19]. ToolGate's contract-grounded preconditions and postconditions provide the nearest technical precedent [18]. The present study operationalized a narrower IT-support workflow around those ideas and added contextual authority routing, endpoint execution, and explicit verification of recovery. The observed rollback also reflects the monitored feedback loop of autonomic computing [20] and controlled-change principles in NIST configuration-management guidance [22].

## 6.3 Contribution and Practical Implications

The principal research contribution is an implemented and evaluated integration of approved support evidence, explainable contextual risk, graded authority, scoped approval, allow-listed execution, observed-state verification, verified recovery or escalation, and audit records. Methodologically, the combination of deterministic scenario experiments, fault injection, paired analysis, repeatability checks, live retrieval, controlled end-to-end cases, and a limited real endpoint made it possible to separate policy behaviour from model variability while still testing the driver boundary.

Practically, the results indicate that selected low-risk operations can avoid unnecessary approval while labelled high-risk operations remain blocked, and that support personnel can receive an explicit record of why an operation ran, failed, recovered, or escalated. The design does not require trusting a generated command or a success message. Its usefulness depends on maintaining correct contracts, catalogue entries, risk configuration, and endpoint observations.

## 6.4 Limitations and Threats to Validity

Construct validity is limited by author-assigned risk, route, safety, and retrieval labels. Their agreement with the implementation measures conformance to the study specification rather than independent policy correctness. The 32 scenarios and 30 articles form small, curated sets; the retrieval evaluation used the correct category and only one live provider run. Changes to the embedding service, missing or incorrect categories, larger knowledge collections, and ambiguous multi-relevant queries were not evaluated.

Internal validity was strengthened by fresh state, fixed inputs, three repeats, retained raw records, independent recalculation, and frozen code. Nevertheless, many end-to-end cases fixed the external model and retriever, so they isolate controller behaviour rather than natural model variation. Condition B is a rubber-stamp simulation, not measured human approval behaviour.

External validity is limited by one Windows endpoint, one operating-system build, synthetic simulator states, and a deliberately staged self-re-registering application. The agent reports its own state without hardware attestation. No recruited users evaluated usability, trust, or organisational effectiveness. Simulator and retrieval-function timings are not end-to-end user latency. The policy weights were not calibrated across organisations, and the persistent audit trail is not cryptographically tamper-evident.

## 6.5 Conclusions

Under the evaluated conditions, AutoOps AI demonstrates that useful IT-support remediation can be integrated with deterministic, risk-adaptive authority and observed-state verification. The framework prevented every labelled unsafe autonomous execution under its complete policy, reduced approval demand relative to approval-all gating, detected every injected fault that executed, refused to treat command success as resolution, and verified recovery on a real Windows endpoint. Retrieval supplied the expected evidence for all evaluated queries, but that perfect closed-set result does not establish general accuracy.

The study therefore answers the central problem with a qualified conclusion: a probabilistic support layer can participate in remediation when execution authority is moved into explicit policy, contracts, scoped authorization, restricted drivers, and post-action evidence. The defensible novelty is the domain-specific integration and evaluation of this loop. Production readiness, universally valid risk policy, and broad endpoint effectiveness have not been demonstrated.

## 6.6 Future Work

Future evaluation should obtain independent expert labels, calibrate risk weights and thresholds, and test a larger knowledge base with missing, incorrect, and uncertain categories. Multi-device and multi-organisation trials should include additional Windows builds and supported operating systems, realistic network failures, longer-running actions, and end-to-end latency. A separately approved participant study could assess whether risk explanations and approval requests are understandable without encouraging rubber-stamp behaviour.

Technical extensions should add independently protected or signed audit records, stronger endpoint attestation, more action-specific rollback contracts, and deployment controls for confidential external-model data. Retrieval could be extended with persistent indexing, freshness and environment metadata, multiple relevance judgements, and answer-faithfulness assessment. These additions should preserve the main boundary established by this study: probabilistic components may interpret and recommend, but deterministic, observable controls must decide and verify operational effects.
