# Abstract

Artificial-intelligence-assisted IT support can improve access to troubleshooting knowledge and automate repetitive operations, but connecting probabilistic language models directly to system tools introduces authorization, operational, and verification risks. This study designed, implemented, and evaluated AutoOps AI, a context-aware IT-support artefact whose principal contribution is an integrated risk-adaptive and verifiable remediation workflow. The framework combines approved-knowledge retrieval and structured action proposals with deterministic contextual risk assessment, graded authorization, action contracts, restricted execution drivers, observed-state postconditions, verified rollback or escalation, and persistent audit records. Design Science Research guided artefact development, while controlled software experiments evaluated specification conformance, comparative policy behaviour, retrieval ranking, failure detection, recovery, and limited endpoint feasibility.

The frozen implementation passed all 518 automated backend tests and all 20 focused end-to-end and prompt/tool-safety tests. Across 288 comparative records derived from 32 scenarios, three conditions, and three repetitions, the risk-adaptive condition executed none of the 36 cases labelled unsafe for autonomous execution, whereas the approval-all simulation executed all 36. It required 72 approval events compared with 96 under approval-all. Every injected fault that reached execution was detected, and none was reported as successful. A live retrieval evaluation ranked the labelled relevant article first for all 30 queries, producing Hit@1 and Hit@3 values of 1.000 and a mean reciprocal rank of 1.000. In the Windows endpoint trial, a command returned successfully but failed its observed postcondition; the framework recorded verified failure, restored the saved state through a registered inverse, verified restoration, and ended in a rolled-back state.

The findings demonstrate specification conformance and controlled feasibility rather than production effectiveness. The evaluation used researcher-labelled scenarios, a small closed retrieval collection, one live embedding run, and one Windows endpoint, and it did not include a participant usability study. Within these boundaries, the study shows how evidence, contextual risk, scoped authority, restricted action, observed outcomes, and verified recovery can be coordinated without treating model-generated text or command completion as sufficient execution authority or proof of resolution.

**Keywords:** AI-assisted IT support, risk-adaptive remediation, retrieval-augmented generation, human authorization, post-action verification, rollback, LLM agents
